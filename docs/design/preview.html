— The idea —

The Workbench: one real part per screen, captioned with the sentence that made it.

Hobbyists don't buy "AI CAD". They want the bracket that snapped, on the bed tonight. So the page shows parts rather than an app window, speaks in millimetres and print minutes rather than adjectives, and keeps every sentence to eight words or fewer.

Suzanne now pitches "Physical AI for industrial design teams". nfinit pitches your desk and your printer.

— Five proposals, one spec —

What each contender contributed

A · Editorial

Parts catalogue

Serif for what people say, sans for what the UI says, mono for what machines measure. Prompts as italic pull-quotes. Dimension-line dividers.

B · Playful

Hot End

Buttons that sink like a key when pressed. Spool-label stickers, capped at three per screen. "New here? Same buttons."

C · Precision

Drawing sheet

Construct blue as the second colour. One part tells the whole story. A dimension value that counts 6.0 → 9.0.

D · Tactile

Specimen

The prompt as caption. A closing prompt field that survives sign-in. The cheap-render performance budget.

E · Luxe

The Hot Dot

The Missing Dot brand mark. Accent as light, never paint. The prompt bar as the hero control. Film grain in midnight.

— Don'ts —
  • No violet, gradient blobs or glass cards
  • No sparkles, wands or "AI-powered" pills
  • No fake app window with traffic lights
  • No centred eyebrow, H1, sub and two pills
  • No icon-in-a-rounded-square feature grid
  • No grey meshes: every part has real mm
  • No accent-filled buttons except the prompt submit
  • No serif under 24px or inside a control
  • No sentence longer than 8 words
